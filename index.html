Handbook

Merging

Drop a tower onto another of the same type and the same level and they become one tower a level higher. Anything else swaps places.

Each merge multiplies damage by ×2.24 and range by ×1.1. Cooldown never changes, so damage per second rises with damage alone. A level 10 tower hits 1,397× harder than a level 1 — and costs 512 of them, so merging always beats spreading out.

Merge levels last for one match. They are gone when the run ends.

Evolutions

Duplicate cards from chests merge into evolutions, two of a level making one of the next, on the Collection tab. Evolutions are permanent — they apply to every run from then on.

Every evolution adds 10%, compounding, so a fully evolved tower is ×2.59. What it improves depends on the tower: damage towers gain damage, farms gain cash, boosters gain a flat +3% boost and +5 range. Range grows 10% per evolution for everyone.

Spawners are the exception. Their allies gain 15% health per evolution rather than 10%, reaching ×4.05 fully evolved, while ally damage stays on the usual 10%.

Evolution 10 needs 1,024 copies of one tower, so treat it as a long chase rather than a target.

Enemies

Crawlers, Grunts, Runners and Brutes walk the lane and do nothing else. Four more arrive later, and each one breaks something your towers rely on.

Moths (wave 11) ignore the path entirely and fly straight from the portal to the base — everything you built along the lane is looking the wrong way. Wasps (16) fly at a tower, stun it for 3 seconds and die doing it; they deal no other damage and pay no bounty, because they were spent rather than killed.

Raiders (21) leave the lane, stun a tower, and walk back on as though nothing happened. They make no progress while they are out there, which is the only thing keeping it fair. Heralds (26) make every tower that can see them shoot them and nothing else — a Quantum covering forty enemies becomes a Quantum covering one.

A stunned tower is ringed in red and its cooldown stops, so three seconds of stun costs three seconds of shooting rather than being partly absorbed by a reload that was happening anyway.

Some towers carry a shield, which blocks one stun and comes back after 20 seconds. Farms, Quantum and the Medic are never stunned at all. Shields stop stuns only — a Herald still takes every shot on the board.

Merging pools what is left of two shields, up to one tower's worth, so a spent shield is still worth bringing to a merge. A Medic clears stuns and refills shields to full on every tower in its reach every second — including towers your party placed.

Bosses

Every tenth wave up to 100, then every fiftieth. A boss wave is the boss and a short escort rather than a full wave on top of it. Its health shows across the top of the board.

Each boss wave has its own, in a fixed order, so wave 10 is always the Warden and wave 80 always the Effigy. There are eight, and they come round again once the list runs out — far enough apart to be worth seeing twice. The run ends at wave 500.

A boss has 25× a brute's health for that wave and moves slowly. It is heavy, so a Fan holds it to about two thirds of its pace rather than a half — but nothing is immune to pushback. Killing one pays 20× a brute's bounty.

Five come round in order, each asking something different of your towers. Warden is immune for 5 seconds, then vulnerable for 8, over and over — it arrives vulnerable, and the bar counts down to each change, so a timestop spent on an open window is worth far more than one spent early. Brood sheds four crawlers every 8 seconds. Cleaver breaks into two smaller bosses when it dies, which do not break again. Sapper halves the fire rate of towers within 6 tiles, so damage spread down the lane keeps working. Wraith heals 4% of its health a second, but only while no tower can reach it — a gap in your coverage is the only thing that feeds it.

Three more carry the newer enemies' tricks at boss scale. Swarmlord flies the straight line and sheds Moths as it goes. Breaker stuns every tower within 5 tiles every 9 seconds, so spreading damage out beats guarding one spot. Effigy takes every shot on the board, which means its escort walks past untouched.

Shinies

One card in a hundred comes out of the chest shiny. A shiny is marked in gold everywhere it appears, and on the board it sits inside a gold ring.

A shiny is a separate line from the normal copy of the same tower. Two shinies merge into a higher shiny, two normals into a higher normal, and the two never mix — in the collection or on the board. Owning both means owning two different towers, and both can go in the loadout at once.

What being shiny is worth depends on the tower. +50% damage for anything that attacks, and for a spawner that lands on its allies. Farms earn +10% cash instead, and boosters give +10% boost — which is worth more than it sounds, since it multiplies across every tower standing in range.

Money and leaks

A cleared wave pays 100 plus every farm's output as the intermission opens, so there is time to spend it. Wave 1 pays nothing — you start with your starting cash only.

Farm income doubles every merge, so a level 10 farm pays 51,200 a wave. Only your best three farms pay, so merging them is the only way income grows.

An enemy that reaches the base costs it whatever health it had left. Wounding something still counts even if it gets through.

Selling refunds half of everything spent building a tower, so a level 5 gives back eight towers' worth.

Controls

1 to 5 pick a hotbar slot. F places the selected tower under the pointer; E places it and keeps the tower selected, so a row goes down without returning to the hotbar between each one.

Q fires the Clock Tower's timestop once it has charged. The button beside the hotbar shows how long is left, and nothing is wasted by waiting — it stays charged until you use it.

Clicking still does everything the keys do. Right click reads a placed tower's full stats, or puts down whatever you are holding. Esc cancels.

Hold a hotbar slot to buy a merged tower outright, once Quick buy is unlocked.
